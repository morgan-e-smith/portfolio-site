## The prediction that didn't come true

In early 2024, Gartner predicted that traditional search volume would fall 25% by 2026 as people turned to AI chatbots instead. Marketers braced for a smaller funnel: fewer searches, fewer visits, less to work with.

It is now 2026, and that drop has not shown up in aggregate search volume. People still search. The funnel did not shrink.

It split. And most B2B marketing teams are still optimizing only the half they can see.

## Discovery moved into AI answers

The top of the funnel, where buyers explore a problem, compare options, and form a view, now happens inside AI tools. In Forrester's latest Buyers' Journey Survey, 94% of business buyers said they use AI in their buying process. More striking, twice as many buyers named generative AI or conversational search their most meaningful information source as named any other, ahead of vendor websites, product experts, and sales.

When the answer arrives in the AI, the click often doesn't happen. Pew Research found that Google users who saw an AI summary clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits, versus 15% when no summary appeared. They clicked a link inside the summary itself just 1% of the time. That is consumer search data, and Google disputes the method, but the direction is hard to ignore.

So the volume is still there. It is just being answered somewhere you don't own.

## The shortlist forms where you can't see it

This would matter less if early research were just browsing. It isn't. 6sense's 2025 Buyer Experience Report found that buyers fill about four of their five shortlist spots on day one of the buying process. Nearly all buying groups rank that shortlist before they contact a single vendor, and the vendor they rank first wins about 80% of the time.

Put that together with the shift to AI, and the uncomfortable conclusion is that many B2B deals are now decided in a conversation the vendor never sees. Your dashboards can show you who visited. They cannot show you the shortlist that formed in a chat window last Tuesday, or the one your brand was left off.

## The website's new job is validation

Here is the good news. Buyers don't fully trust what the AI tells them. Forrester's 2026 State of Business Buying report notes that AI answer engines often give incomplete or unreliable information, so buyers go looking for validation from sources they trust. Gartner found in May 2026 that 69% of B2B buyers prefer to validate AI-generated insights with a salesperson.

That is where the bottom half of the funnel lives now. Buyers who reach your website are rarely there to learn what you do. The AI already told them. They are there to check whether it was right.

And those visitors are worth more. Microsoft Clarity's study of more than 1,200 publisher sites found AI-referred visitors converted to sign-ups at 1.66%, against 0.15% from organic search, even though AI drove under 1% of traffic. Adobe found AI-referred retail visitors converting 42% better than non-AI visitors by March 2026. Neither study is B2B, so treat the numbers as directional. The pattern is consistent: fewer visitors, arriving further along and readier to act.

## One site can't do both jobs

Most B2B websites were built for a buyer who arrived early and needed educating. The homepage introduced the company, the navigation mapped its services, and the content explained capabilities from the ground up.

That buyer is now mostly in an AI tool. The two halves of the funnel need completely different things, and trying to make one site serve both is how you end up serving neither. For the validation half, a few principles follow:

- **Every page is a home page.** AI-referred visitors tend to land deep in a site, not on the front door. Any page a buyer lands on has to stand on its own.
- **Proof over pitch.** A buyer checking an AI answer wants evidence: outcomes, specifics, and examples of the exact problem solved before. Capability statements don't validate anything.
- **Organize around the buyer's problem, not your org chart.** People arrive with a challenge in mind. Structure built around internal service names makes them translate.
- **Write for two readers.** The same content now has to persuade a person and be clear, structured, and citable enough for an AI to repeat accurately. If the AI gets you wrong upstream, the validation visit never happens.

## Where to start

None of this requires rebuilding a website overnight. It starts with three moves:

1. **Make the invisible visible.** Separate AI-referred traffic in your analytics. Many platforms still lump it in with direct traffic, so most teams don't know how big it is or how it behaves.
2. **Ask the AI about yourself.** Run the questions your buyers ask through the major AI tools and read what comes back. Whether your brand appears, and how accurately, is your new top of funnel.
3. **Pilot small.** Pick one business area, restructure its content for validation and AI citation, and measure. A bounded test earns the case for a bigger one.

And when you take this to leadership, frame it as an opportunity, not a failure. Nobody wants to fund a fix for the site they built. Everyone wants to fund capturing the highest-intent buyers in the market.

The funnel didn't shrink. It split. The brands that design for both halves will win the shortlists the rest of us never see.

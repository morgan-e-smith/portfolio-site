# Morgan Smith

Atelier: crafted strategy, built with AI.

This is the personal brand system for Morgan Smith's professional portfolio. Morgan is an AI and marketing transformation leader who sets the strategy, builds the tools, and drives the change. The brand should feel like a maker's workshop: warm, precise, and confident, with one bright accent that signals energy and a sense of play. It draws on two things Morgan loves outside work, the mountains and jewelry making, without ever spelling them out.

## Personality

Crafted, sharp, warm. Senior but never stiff. The work does the bragging; the brand just frames it well.

- **Crafted, not corporate.** Details are considered. Nothing looks templated.
- **Sharp, not loud.** One accent color, used sparingly, carries all the energy.
- **Warm, not soft.** Ivory paper and brass instead of stark white and chrome.

## Voice and content

- **Lead with the outcome.** "100% onboarded to the enterprise AI platform," not "responsible for platform onboarding."
- **Direct and a little witty.** "AI didn't run these programs. I did, using AI."
- **Honest status.** Work in progress says so, with an "In progress" tag. Never overclaim.
- **Plain words.** No jargon, no buzzword stacks. Explain acronyms on first use.
- **No em dashes.** Use a period, comma, or colon instead.
- **Sentence case** for all headings and buttons.
- **Anonymize employer specifics** in case studies: "a global professional services firm." Name the employer only on About and Resume.

## Color

Paper and ink do almost all the work. Accents are seasoning.

- **Ground:** `paper` for pages, `paper-raised` for cards. Text is `ink`; secondary text is `ink-muted`.
- **Signature accent:** turquoise, on 5 to 10% of any screen. Use `turquoise-ink` for anything people read or click: links, primary buttons, focus rings, and the one italic accent word in a headline. Use `turquoise` only for large fills and decorative shapes, never for text on paper. Tags use `turquoise-tint` with `turquoise-ink` text.
- **Second accent:** `brass` for hairline rules, the monogram, and contour lines. When brass must be read (eyebrows, small labels), use `brass-ink`.
- **Structure:** `line` for borders and dividers; `silver` for icon strokes and disabled states.
- **Never:** purple, orange, red, or pink anywhere in the system. No gradients.

## Typography

Two families: Fraunces (serif) for display, Inter (sans) for everything else. Both load from Google Fonts.

- **Display** (`display-xl`, `display-l`, `heading`) in Fraunces 500. Headlines may italicize ONE word in `turquoise-ink`, the word that carries the point, e.g. "I get AI from pilot to *practice*." One per headline, never more, and not in every headline on a page.
- **Text** (`body`, `body-s`) in Inter 400. Body copy runs 60 to 75 characters per line.
- **Eyebrow** in Inter 600, uppercase, letter-spaced, in `brass-ink`. Used for section labels and pillar tags.

## Signature touches

1. **The italic accent word** in display headlines (see Typography).
2. **Topographic contour lines** as a faint texture in the hero and as section dividers: three to five thin `brass` lines at low opacity. A quiet nod to the mountains.
3. **The MS monogram**, in `brass`, styled like a maker's stamp. Used as the favicon and the footer sign-off. *Status: not yet designed. Until it exists, use the name set in Fraunces.*

## Layout and shape

- Generous whitespace: `space-5` between sections on desktop, `space-4` between blocks.
- Cards are `paper-raised` with a 1px `line` border and `radius-md`. No shadows, no colored left borders.
- Pills (`radius-pill`) are for filter chips and pillar tags only.
- Mobile first. Most recruiters open the site from a phone first.

## Iconography

Outline icons with a 1.5px stroke in `ink` or `silver`, never filled, never emoji. Icons support labels; they never replace them.

## Accessibility

All text meets 4.5:1 on its background; usage notes give each pair's ratio. `turquoise`, `brass`, and `silver` fail as text on paper by design, so use their `-ink` versions. Focus rings are 2px `turquoise-ink`.

## Don't

- Don't use the accent for body text or large text blocks.
- Don't add a third accent color.
- Don't use stock photos, AI-generated people, or emoji.
- Don't reuse any former employer's brand colors or fonts.
